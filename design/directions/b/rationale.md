# Direction B — Count-In

*Bold rhythmic graphic · dark-first (full light theme) · Archivo / JetBrains Mono*

## The idea in three sentences

Build the brand out of rhythm itself: poster-weight Archivo set like a gig flyer, thick
rules that read as barlines, chapters as tracks, lessons as pads on a step grid, a red
downbeat and an amber LED as the only two accents on a stage-black ground. Dark is the
default theme because drummers practice at night and screens are stages — and the one lit
object on that stage is the score, which keeps its paper ground in both themes. The
metronome becomes the transport: a full-width dock on the bottom edge of every page,
exactly where a musician's hands expect it.

## How it says "drum.town / everyone in it together"

Through the count-in — the most communal gesture in music: "we'll count you in" is the
hero, the downbeat is marked red everywhere the beat appears, and the whole town
practicing "to the same click" is the promise the footer repeats ("Keep time. Keep it
free."). Togetherness is expressed as shared time rather than simulated presence — no
fake avatars, no invented activity, just the language and iconography of playing on the
same one.

## What it sacrifices

Almost all of the current site's bookish gentleness: serifs, cream quietness, and the
manuscript mood are gone, which risks reading "merch drop" to a visitor who loved the
library feel, and beginners could find the poster voice more intense than welcoming.
Uppercase display type also spends legibility on attitude, so it must stay strictly at
display sizes — body text and notation do the patient work.

## How current users experience the transition

This is the loudest of the three transitions: a returning drummer lands on a black page
with poster type and will immediately know the site changed — the design has to repay
that shock in the first five seconds, which is why "Continue where you left off" sits
directly under the hero and their pads arrive pre-lit from existing localStorage. What
they rely on daily actually gets calmer: the metronome stops floating over notation
(BL-036) and locks to the bottom edge like a transport bar, the score panel is *brighter*
and easier to read than before in a dark room, and on the phone the lesson — not the
sidebar — is the first screen (BL-037). Anyone who prefers the old warmth has a one-tap
daylight theme that keeps the same bones on paper instead of black.

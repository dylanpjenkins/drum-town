# Direction C — The Woodshed

*Quiet craft studio · light-first (full "after hours" dark theme) · Newsreader / IBM Plex Sans / IBM Plex Mono*

## The idea in three sentences

"Woodshedding" is drummers' own word for real practice, so the town's shared space is a
practice room with the door open: stone and felt grounds, walnut and brass hardware, graph
paper under the hero, and the curriculum set as a practice-journal ledger with dotted
leaders. Newsreader carries the prose the way the current serif does, but the room around
it is workshop-precise — hairline rules, one 6px radius, IBM Plex labels — craft rather
than manuscript. The metronome is a practice clock with a reserved seat: a real layout
gutter at desktop widths, a bookmark tab at the page edge below them.

## How it says "drum.town / everyone in it together"

Quietly, the way a shared rehearsal space does: "a practice room with the door open,"
"everyone in town works from the same book," "practice alone, together," a colophon
footer instead of boilerplate, and progress marked like pencil ticks in a communal
logbook (✓ done / ● here). The town is a place you can hear other people practicing
through the wall — implied by voice and framing, never simulated with fake presence.

## What it sacrifices

Immediate wow: this direction has the least poster energy of the three, and a visitor
skimming for five seconds may read "tasteful" before "new" — it banks on the second visit,
not the first. It also keeps a serif-forward look that sits closer to the current site
than A or B, so it does the least to signal "rebrand" and relies on structure (ledger,
clock, journal) to carry the change.

## How current users experience the transition

For today's users this is the gentlest possible move that still fixes everything: prose
stays serif and generous, cream becomes stone, rust becomes moss and brass, and their
lessons now sit in a ledger where finished rows carry a ✓ and the current row a "● here"
mark drawn from the progress they already have. The annoyances disappear without
ceremony — the metronome no longer parks on top of a stave at 1280px (BL-036) because the
layout genuinely reserves its corner, the phone shows the lesson first with the map folded
to one line (BL-037), and the wrapping dash clutter in chapter lists is gone because the
ledger has no separators to strand (BL-044). The dark theme will feel made for them the
first late night they practice: same room, lights low, score still paper-white.

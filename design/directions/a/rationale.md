# Direction A — The Square

*Warm civic modern · light-first (full dark theme) · Fraunces / Public Sans / IBM Plex Mono*

## The idea in three sentences

Take the town metaphor literally and build the interface as civic infrastructure: the
curriculum is the map, chapters are streets with numbered stops, progress is "streets
you've walked," and the metronome is the clock tower on the skyline — permanently in the
sticky header, never floating over the street. The manuscript's warmth survives as
material (plaster, brick, brass, park green) but the typography flips from archival to
public: Fraunces for warmth at display sizes, Public Sans — a typeface literally designed
for civic communication — for everything you read and press. Every screen answers "where
am I, and where do I go next?" the way good wayfinding does.

## How it says "drum.town / everyone in it together"

The town is the information architecture, not a slogan: a seal in the masthead, "The Map"
as the nav's first word, "you are here" markers, a legend for walked streets, one shared
clock in every page's header, and a footer that reads like a notice board ("The door's
open. Bring sticks."). Communal voice with zero fake community — the copy says plainly
that progress lives in your browser and nothing is tracked, which *is* the town's promise:
public, free, no gatekeeper.

## What it sacrifices

The hushed, private-manuscript exclusivity of the current site — Cormorant's fragile
elegance is traded for friendlier, sturdier letterforms, and some of the "rare book"
mystique goes with it. It also spends its accent budget on wayfinding states (visited /
here / next), so it has less room for expressive, poster-scale graphic moments than
Direction B; the drama here is civic, not theatrical.

## How current users experience the transition

A returning drummer lands on the same cream warmth and the same rust family, so nothing
feels like a hostile takeover — but the page now greets them with "Continue where you left
off" instead of a title card, their finished lessons read as walked streets with green
stamps, and the metronome they already rely on has moved from a card that sometimes sat on
top of the notation (BL-036) to a pill in the header that is always in the same place at
every width. The lesson they open has the same prose and the same staves, just one
breadcrumb and one chip row where four stacked labels used to be, and on their phone the
lesson itself — not the sidebar — is the first thing on screen (BL-037). The likely
reaction is not "redesign" but "the place got signage."
